Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Loud popping, banging, or rumbling from the tank that has gotten worse quickly
  • A puddle under the tank that keeps growing
  • A T&P valve that releases water repeatedly instead of staying closed
  • Hot water far hotter than normal, a sign of a runaway thermostat or gas control
  • A breaker that trips every time the electric heater tries to run
  • Any gas odor around the heater, which means getting out and calling the gas company before calling us
  • Black soot or heat discoloration around the burner door or draft hood
  • A complete loss of hot water throughout the house

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

It is tempting to treat a leaking or dead water heater as a quick fix. In reality, you may be dealing with standing water beside 240-volt wiring, a gas supply and live burner a few inches from a puddle, and a tank of very hot water under pressure. The relief valve, if it is firing, is the final line of defense between an overheated tank and a violent failure, and plugging it is one of the most dangerous things anyone can do to a heater. Relighting a pilot while gas lingers, or cranking on a corroded fitting until it snaps, can make things much worse. Evacuate for gas, isolate, then phone is the right sequence for anyone without the tools and training.

Professional help is not just about having wrenches. It is about knowing why the failure happened so it does not happen again next month. When a relief valve discharges, we measure static water pressure and look for a missing or failed expansion tank. When a tank floods the floor, we locate the true source, whether it is the valve, a fitting, a connector, or the tank wall. When a gas heater dies, we check the thermocouple or flame sensor, gas pressure, and venting before restarting it. The diagnosis determines everything that follows. Components can be replaced the same visit; a failed tank cannot be patched, and we say so plainly and explain the replacement path.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Test the T&P valve annually; a valve that sticks or weeps afterward should be replaced
  • Put the heater in a drain pan piped to a floor drain or the exterior where the setup allows
  • Keep the thermostat near 120F to limit scalding and reduce strain on the tank
  • Tag the water heater supply valve and the main shutoff so nobody has to search during a leak
  • Flush sediment from the tank yearly, since buildup overheats the bottom and speeds corrosion
  • Consider an automatic leak shutoff that stops the cold supply the moment water hits the floor
  • Keep boxes, paint, gasoline, and cleaning products well away from a gas water heater
