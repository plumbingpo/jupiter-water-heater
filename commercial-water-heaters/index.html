Warning Signs to Watch For

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Loud sediment noise from equipment that runs all day
  • Trouble reaching sanitation temperatures in the kitchen
  • A heater old enough that a failure feels like a matter of time
  • Visible corrosion or water pooling near the tank
  • Energy costs climbing while usage stays flat
  • Unsteady delivery temperatures that suggest a failing mixing valve
  • A recirculation loop that no longer seems to do its job
  • The tank giving up in the middle of a rush

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here's where to begin:

  • Check anodes on a schedule matched to your volume and water quality
  • Keep recirculation pumps and check valves in service to avoid long waits and wasted energy
  • Schedule upgrades during slow periods instead of waiting for a breakdown
  • Have mixing valves checked so fixtures stay neither scalding nor lukewarm
  • Track temperatures at the dish machine and hand sinks as part of routine checks
  • Do not store supplies against the equipment or block air openings
  • Flush commercial tanks more often than residential ones, often every few months in heavy use or hard water
