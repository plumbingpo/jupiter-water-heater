Most gas water heaters in Jupiter, FL work the same basic way. Gas enters through a control valve that doubles as the thermostat and feeds a burner beneath the tank, and the hot exhaust travels up a flue through the middle of the tank and out through the vent. A thermocouple, or a flame sensor on newer electronic models, tells the valve a flame is present; without that signal, the valve shuts off the gas for safety. The design is dependable, but it has a few predictable weak spots. Thermocouples wear out, pilot tubes and burner ports clog, the flame arrestor screen at the base packs up with lint and dust, control valves fail, and vents lose their draft.

Jupiter Water Heater does not guess with gas appliances. On every Jupiter call, our technicians test the thermocouple's millivolt output, inspect the pilot and burner flame, check gas pressure at the valve, and run a draft test at the draft hood to confirm exhaust is actually leaving the house. We look inside the combustion chamber for scorching, rust, and signs of flame rollout, which tell us whether the flue is blocked or the burner is misbehaving, and we check for carbon monoxide around the heater. One rule comes before all of it: if you smell gas, do not light anything or flip switches. Leave the house, call your gas utility from outside, and then call us once it is safe.

The fix is usually straightforward once the cause is known. A new thermocouple or flame sensor, a cleaned pilot tube and burner, a cleared flame arrestor screen, or a replacement gas control valve gets most heaters running again the same day. Vent problems, like a disconnected connector or a flue pipe without enough rise, are corrected on the spot. If sediment is rumbling in the tank, we flush it, and if the anode rod is spent, we can replace it. When replacement is the smarter move, we install new gas heaters sized to your household's first-hour demand, with proper atmospheric, power vent, or direct vent venting, a drip leg on the gas supply, and the expansion tank, drain pan, and strapping your local code calls for. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A yellow, lazy, or sooty burner flame instead of a crisp blue one
  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • Pilot outages that keep coming back after a relight
  • No hot water at all, with the burner never firing
  • Any smell of gas around the appliance, which calls for leaving and phoning the gas utility
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater
  • Loud rumbling from the bottom of the tank
  • A vent pipe that has separated, rusted, or left scorch marks near the top of the heater

Don't Wait for a Total Failure

Most water heater failures start small: a popping sound from sediment, a damp spot under the drain valve, or a shower that goes lukewarm a few minutes sooner each week. If you notice any of these signs, contact Jupiter Water Heater at (561) 532-3581 for a prompt inspection.

Why Professional Gas Water Heater Services Matters

Gas water heaters are among the riskiest appliances to work on without training, largely because you cannot see the biggest hazards. A fitting that is almost tight can seep gas for days before anyone notices. A vent connector that has lost its slope, or a flue partly blocked by debris, can let carbon monoxide spill into the house every time the burner fires, with no smell to warn you. And many repairs, including gas valve and heater replacement, require a permit and inspection in most areas. Relighting a pilot by following the label is something most homeowners can do; almost everything past that deserves a technician.

The value of calling a pro is certainty. We test instead of guessing, from thermocouple millivolt readings and burner flame checks to gas pressure at the valve and a draft test at the hood. Before we leave, every connection is leak-tested, the vent is confirmed to carry exhaust outside, and CO is checked. And because some gas heaters are not worth another repair, we give you a straight answer on repair versus replacement first, then handle either one with correct venting, gas piping, and the code items your installation requires.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years and replaced when it is worn
  • Check that the vent connector is secure and rising toward the chimney or flue
  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve periodically to control sediment
  • Set the gas valve near 120 degrees, because hotter water costs more and can scald
  • Keep working CO alarms near bedrooms and on each floor
  • Never store flammable liquids near a gas water heater
  • Clean dust from around the base so the burner gets plenty of combustion air
