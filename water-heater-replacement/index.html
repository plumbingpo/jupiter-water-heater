Signs You Need Water Heater Replacement in Jupiter

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Water seeping out from under the tank jacket
  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • Banging and popping from a tank packed with sediment
  • Less and less hot water, even after repairs
  • Visible corrosion around the pipe connections or burner area
  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old
  • Repeated service calls on the same aging heater
  • Higher gas or electric bills for less hot water

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Replacement

Swapping a water heater looks simple until you count the connections. Gas units need a leak-free connection with a sediment trap, a correctly sized and pitched vent that sends carbon monoxide outdoors, and enough combustion air. Electric units need a breaker and circuit that match the element wattage. Every tank needs a correctly rated relief valve with a discharge pipe routed to a safe spot, and many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps to meet local code. Going tankless or switching to a heat pump adds even more: bigger gas lines, different vent materials, dedicated circuits, and condensate drains.

A professional replacement covers the whole installation, not just the tank. Sizing is done by first-hour rating or flow rate. Fuel, venting, and electrical capacity are verified, and whatever expansion tank, drain pan, or straps your home needs get added. We leak-test every joint, check draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and haul the old heater off. The result is a heater that is safe, sized for your household, and ready for inspection if one is required. If your current tank still has good years left and just needs a part, we will tell you directly.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Keep household water pressure in a safe range to protect the tank and its fittings
  • Drain a few gallons every six months so sediment never gets a foothold
  • Avoid running the tank hotter than about 120 degrees unless there is a specific need
  • Plan ahead: knowing the heater's age lets you replace it on your schedule, not after a leak
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Clean the air filter on a heat pump water heater every few months
  • Inspect the anode rod every few years; replacing it can add years to the tank
