Clear Pricing

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Jupiter Water Heater provides honest, upfront pricing for every home, covering everything from a failed heating element to a full tank replacement. Every visit begins with a careful inspection of your unit, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and valve and tank condition, followed by a plain-English recommendation with the price spelled out before any work starts.

The problem with water heater failures is that many of them develop out of sight. Minerals build a crust on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod wears down a little at a time, and once it's gone, the tank itself starts to corrode. When hot water comes out rusty, the tank rumbles, or a small drip appears at the base, get it looked at while it's still a simple repair.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Many homeowners call us to size up an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with regular descaling), a replacement on your own timeline beats a rushed swap after a leak. We'll give you an honest read on how much life your unit has left.

Maintenance Plans

Maintenance Plans for Jupiter Homes

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing year-round. Regular service prevents failures before they start, flushes out mineral sediment, and catches small faults while they're still cheap to fix. At Jupiter Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your specific equipment, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a burner and flue check on gas units, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a temperature check at the tap.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and twice-yearly flushing where the water is hard. That's especially important where mineral content is high, where sediment coats the tank floor, forces the burner to run longer, and wears out the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your heater's age, your household's hot water demands, and your local water.

Consider maintenance as protection for equipment you've already bought. Each visit helps keep the tank clear of sediment, the anode rod working, and the relief valve ready to open, which lowers monthly energy bills while reducing wear on the burner or elements. For most households, maintenance costs far less than replacing the heater ahead of schedule.

Sized for Your Household

The Right Size Water Heater for Your Home

An undersized water heater runs out halfway through the second shower, while an oversized one wastes energy keeping gallons you never use hot around the clock. Jupiter Water Heater sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual household. For tank models, that means looking at the first-hour rating to your busiest hour, not just copying the gallon size on the old tank. For tankless models, it means sizing the gallons per minute to the fixtures you run at once and how cold your incoming water gets in winter.

Picking a new water heater comes down to more than tank size. We'll compare tankless units against a traditional tank, and gas against electric or hybrid, walking through operating costs, venting and gas line requirements, electrical capacity, and the installation itself. Tankless is a strong fit for many families, but it often needs a larger gas line and new venting, and a properly sized tank is often the simpler, less expensive option. The decision is yours, based on your home and budget.

Frequent Failures

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

Homes around Jupiter run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Jupiter Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that only gets lukewarm, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks that trip the breaker, noisy tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

The right repair depends entirely on what actually failed. On electric tanks, that usually means an element or thermostat. On gas units, it's more often a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure problems are solved with a thermal expansion tank and a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and we handle leak repairs at the drain valve and fittings before the damage spreads. We also check the thermostat setting, since water above 120°F can scald young children and older adults in seconds. A tank leaking from the steel itself can't be patched, and we'll tell you so. And if you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility right away.
